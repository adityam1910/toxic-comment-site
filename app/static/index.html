    Three models, two ways of reading

    Logistic Regression and Naive Bayes read the same TF-IDF features (single words and word pairs) and train one binary classifier per label. The BiLSTM reads the comment as a sequence of words, forwards and backwards, and scores all six labels at once.

    Read the scores with care

    A score is a signal for a person to review, not a judgement about a comment or the person who wrote it.

    The “false alarm” example above is real. “i like your dress” is flagged as toxic by Logistic Regression and Naive Bayes. The cleaning step removes common words such as “i”, “you” and “your”, and in the training data comments addressed to “you” are more often toxic, so a harmless compliment can look suspicious. The BiLSTM scores the same comment at 48%, which is below its toxic threshold, so it is not flagged. That is one comment, not proof that it is more accurate.

    The models were trained on English Wikipedia talk-page comments. They can misread sarcasm, quotes, slang and mentions of identity groups. Comments you enter are scored in memory and are not stored.

    About this project

    Built by Aditya Raj Mathur at Manipal University Jaipur, Department of Computer and Communication Engineering, under the guidance of Dr. Anshika Malsaria.

    The project studies class imbalance in multilabel toxic-comment detection. In a matched BiLSTM experiment, weighting the loss for rare labels raised test macro-F1 from 0.4057 to 0.5469 at a 0.5 threshold, with lower micro-F1. That was a single run, so it is a finding for one setup and not a general claim.
